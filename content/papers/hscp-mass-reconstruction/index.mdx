## Overview

During a US-CMS PURSUE internship at Fermilab, working with Dr. Emery Nibigira
and the CMS-EXO-HSCP group, I benchmarked several ways of estimating specific
ionization — dE/dx — per track, and compared what each one does to the
reconstructed mass of a heavy stable charged particle.

The sample was a 1800 GeV gluino, 2018 CMS NanoAOD at 13 TeV, generated with
PYTHIA 8.240 under the CP5 tune and NNPDF3.1.

## Motivation

Nearly everything produced at the LHC is relativistic, which makes it useless
for this purpose: at high βγ a particle's energy loss sits near the
minimum-ionizing plateau, where dE/dx is almost flat and carries very little
information about what the particle is.

A heavy, slow particle is the exception. It loses substantially more energy per
unit length, and the size of that excess depends on βγ — so combined with the
momentum the tracker already measures, dE/dx becomes a handle on mass. This is
what makes a search for something heavy and long-lived possible at all.

The difficulty is that energy loss in a single silicon hit is not a
well-behaved quantity. It follows a Landau distribution, with a long tail on
the high side, and a track supplies only a modest number of hits. So the
per-track number is an estimate from a small, skewed sample, and the choice of
estimator is not bookkeeping — it sets the mass resolution, and the resolution
sets the sensitivity.

## Method

I implemented and compared three estimators:

- **Harmonic-2 mean** — the CMS baseline, and more robust to the Landau tail
  than an arithmetic mean.
- **Adaptive truncated mean** — drops a fraction of the highest-ionizing hits,
  with the fraction scaling to the number of hits on the track rather than
  being fixed. A short track cannot afford to lose as much as a long one.
- **Per-track Landau-MPV fit** — an adaptively binned Landau fit per track
  using ROOT's MINUIT/MIGRAD, taking the most probable value as the estimate.
  Rather than trying to be robust against the tail, this one models it.

Around the estimators sits the less interesting and equally necessary half:
`isHighPurity` track selection, removal of non-physical `Ih ≤ 0` values,
filtering of detector edge effects, and per-track convergence monitoring so a
failed fit is discarded rather than quietly returned. Diagnostics were run
against both track momentum and pseudorapidity, since an estimator that is
unbiased overall can still be wrong in a corner of the detector.

## Results

The Landau-MPV fit converged on roughly **89.6% of about 130,000 tracks** —
which is the first thing worth knowing about it, because an estimator that
fails on one track in ten is a different proposition from one that always
returns something.

Its reconstructed mass distribution showed a **secondary peak**. I want to be
careful about this: in a prototype fit, a second peak is a thing to explain
before it is a thing to celebrate. It may point at a genuine population, or at
a failure mode of the fit under particular track conditions. Distinguishing
those is exactly what the remaining systematic work is for, and I did not get
that far.

Cumulative efficiency against dE/dx cut was tabulated for comparison across
estimators.

## Motivation

{/*
  Your call how much to explain. The shape of it, to check against:
  - most LHC particles are relativistic → dE/dx sits near the minimum-ionizing
    plateau → carries little mass information
  - a heavy, slow particle ionizes much more; the excess depends on βγ, so with
    tracker momentum you get mass
  - but per-hit energy loss is Landau-distributed (long high tail) and a track
    gives few hits → the per-track estimate is noisy → choice of estimator sets
    the mass resolution, which sets search sensitivity
  - say why this matters for an HSCP search specifically — that's yours
*/}

## Method

{/*
  Three estimators benchmarked:
  - harmonic-2 mean (CMS baseline; robust to Landau tails vs arithmetic mean)
  - adaptive truncated mean — fraction of high-ionizing hits dropped scales
    with hit count / track length
  - per-track Landau-MPV fit — adaptively binned Landau fit, ROOT MINUIT/MIGRAD,
    MPV taken as dE/dx. Convergence monitored, non-physical results filtered.

  Cleaning / selection:
  - isHighPurity selection
  - remove Ih ≤ 0
  - filter detector edge effects

  Diagnostics run vs track momentum (p) and pseudorapidity (η).

  Stack: ROOT/PyROOT, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, Jupyter, Conda (env.yml),
  Makefile for automation.

  Repo modules: notebooks/ (estimator comparisons, mass reco, ROC curves),
  src/, output/, cumulative_efficiencies.csv
*/}
